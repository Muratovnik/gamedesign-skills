# How Game Design relates to existing approaches

**Status: current.**

Game Design keeps familiar concerns such as feedback loops, progression,
accessibility and the relation between implementation and player experience.
It adds procedures for applying those concerns to a concrete game artifact,
checking the changed relation and keeping a lawful alternative in view. This is
a design rationale, not a ranking of frameworks or a measured comparison of
agents.

The [MDA paper by Hunicke, LeBlanc and Zubek](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~hunicke/MDA.pdf)
separates implemented mechanics, behavior over time and desired player
responses. It connects implementation and experience through analysis,
playtesting and tuning. Its aesthetic vocabulary is not a universal formula
for fun. Game Design uses the same broad distinction when it separates a
recorded technical action from a player's interpretation of that action.

Compact game-design skill collections can also orient a reader to loops,
progression, prototyping and accessibility. For example, see the public
[game-design skill in agentic-awesome-skills](https://github.com/sickn33/agentic-awesome-skills/blob/main/skills/game-development/game-design/SKILL.md).
Its topic reminders can be useful at the point of entry. Game Design keeps
constructive procedures for revising an actual artifact and does not require a
design document or victory condition for every task.

These approaches inform the package's context but are not redistributed or
used as templates. Their general ideas do not establish that Game Design
improves model decisions or human play. That requires evidence from the relevant
people, games and tools.
