# Primary research and evidence limits

**Status: current.**

Use this page to understand what the package's source material supports and
where a design claim needs evidence from a particular game or its players.
Research can justify a useful distinction; it does not establish that every
design using that distinction will work.

The [MDA paper by Hunicke, LeBlanc and Zubek](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~hunicke/MDA.pdf)
distinguishes implemented mechanics, behavior over time and desired player
responses. It describes analysis, playtesting and tuning as ways to connect
implementation to experience. Its aesthetic vocabulary is a lens for discussion,
not a universal formula for fun. Game Design uses the same broad separation when
it distinguishes a recorded technical action from a player's interpretation of
that action.

## Transfer a source claim carefully

For a specific design choice, follow the method's linked primary research or
practice source and retain its population, setting and measured outcome. Then
state which part informs the current game and which differences could change the
result. The individual method references provide those source links and their
transfer conditions; this page does not duplicate their inventories.

The examples supply different kinds of evidence. A paper replay follows stated
rules and assumptions. A schema check validates structure. A deterministic
consumer can establish that a declared input produces a declared state change.
An engine trace concerns its tested fixture and engine behavior. These results
can expose a broken relation, but they do not show that people noticed, understood
or valued the change.

## Keep claims within the evidence

Claims about enjoyment, learning, cultural interpretation, accessibility in use
or social consent require observations appropriate to those questions. A
written fictional testimony is authored game content, not empirical testimony.
Likewise, the presence of a method does not establish that a model will select
or apply it, or that the package improves results. Those claims require
comparable evidence from relevant tasks, games and tools.

The authored examples are synthetic. Their technical and arithmetic records
describe only the declared cases; they are not player studies, general engine
support claims, or undisclosed final evaluation data. For the mapping from a
capability to its method and example, use the [capability map](traceability.md).

The [source-pinned external comparison](research/2026-10-08/comparison.md) and
[verification record](reviews/2026-10-08-comparison/README.md) separate discovered
patterns, retained terms and observed operations. The supplied discovery report
was not accepted as licensing or performance evidence. Six explicit-source model
pairs remain a bounded diagnostic comparison, not proof of native discovery or
general improvement.
