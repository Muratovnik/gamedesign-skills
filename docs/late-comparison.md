# Primary research and evidence limits

**Status: current.**

Game Design connects design decisions to concrete game artifacts, their
behavior and the experience they are intended to support. Its references cite
the primary research or practice behind particular distinctions and state the
conditions under which those distinctions transfer.

The [MDA paper by Hunicke, LeBlanc and Zubek](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~hunicke/MDA.pdf)
separates implemented mechanics, behavior over time and desired player
responses. It connects implementation and experience through analysis,
playtesting and tuning. Its aesthetic vocabulary is not a universal formula
for fun. Game Design uses the same broad distinction when it separates a
recorded technical action from a player's interpretation of that action.

Research can support a distinction without establishing the quality of every
game made with it. A technical trace can show that an action changed state;
whether people noticed, understood or valued that change requires the relevant
observations. Likewise, the presence of a method does not establish that a model
selects or applies it, or that the package improves its results. Those claims
need comparable evidence from the relevant tasks, games and tools.

The [2026-10-08 external comparison](research/2026-10-08/comparison.md) applies
that standard to exact public methods, implementations, tests and terms. It
records the useful components as well as counterexamples and deferred routes,
and links the requirement/plan changes to the implemented consumers. Its
[verification record](reviews/2026-10-08-comparison/README.md) distinguishes
script tests, native lifecycle, paired model tasks and independent review. The
supplied discovery report is not used as verified licensing or performance
evidence.
