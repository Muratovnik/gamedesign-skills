# Lens workshop

An original tabletop teaching episode for one learner and one facilitator, or
an author replay. The explicit external objective is comparing fractions with
different denominators. The game asks for the lot with the smaller fraction of
cracked lenses. Every lens in these fictional lots has been inspected; this is
not a claim about a sample's estimate of future production.

Use the four pairs of cards below, a choice token and a notebook. The learner
may read the counts, request a spoken readout, use arithmetic, draw groups or
ask what “cracked fraction” means. The definition “cracked count divided by total
count” and readout remain available throughout. In each round commit left,
right or equal before revealing the comparison. One correct decision earns one
repair marker for that round. Either lot or equal earns a marker when the rates
tie. There is no speed requirement. The session ends after four rounds or on
request, with the obtained markers recorded.

| Round | Left card | Right card | Reveal after commitment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Introduction | 2 cracked / 10 total | 3 cracked / 30 total | Right: 1/10 is less than 1/5. Repeat the left ten-lens group three times to see 6/30. |
| New case | 4 cracked / 40 total | 2 cracked / 10 total | Left: 1/10 is less than 1/5, despite having more cracks in total. |
| Equal-size contrast | 5 cracked / 20 total | 1 cracked / 20 total | Right: 1/20 is less than 1/4; equal denominators permit comparing counts. |
| Tie | 1 cracked / 10 total | 2 cracked / 20 total | Equal: both are 1/10. |

In the supported introduction, the facilitator may work the comparison together
with the learner. For the new case, present both counts without a choosing
highlight. Keep the permanent definition and any agreed reading assistance.
Someone who already knows the relation may skip the introduction and begin
with the new case; this does not remove access to any needed tool.

## What changed

The original weak version highlights Right in the introduction and awards a
“ratio expert” badge after that click. It is possible to satisfy that rule without
comparing anything. The altered version changes the correct side and denominator
in a later application, includes a same-size contrast and accepts ties. It awards
only the observed round decision. The smaller-raw-count heuristic selects Right
in the new case and therefore fails it; that is a concrete distinguishing case.

After the rounds, the intended discussion is:

1. Why did two cracked lenses mean different fractions in different groups?
2. When was comparing raw counts enough, and why?
3. What did the game tell us about these fully inspected lots? What additional
   information would be needed to predict a future batch?

The discussion can itself be the educational use of an entertaining game. If
the brief selects that role, the game need not force the subject operation into
every winning move. Here, guessing or memorizing the published cards still can
produce correct choices. The authored relation and the arithmetic checks do not
establish acquired understanding. Transfer beyond the game would require an
actual new task and actual learner evidence, neither supplied by this example.
