# Relations and current context

Use for a new direction, a disputed design problem or a change crossing subject
boundaries. Skip a new whole-game framing when the local relation is already set.

## Construct the relation

Translate a broad intention into an encounterable situation. For example,
"a port remembers its people" can become a returned tool carrying its former
owner's repair marks, a changed route maintained by a community, or a rule that
lets a new crew inherit obligations. These create different actions and costs;
they are not three names for a reputation bar. A world can express memory through
authored places without simulating every resident.

Use only the context that changes the decision:

| Question | Material to recover | Consequence for construction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What is valued here? | Current brief, accepted constraints and intended audience | Choose what may change and what must survive |
| What can this participant know and do? | Visible facts, control and assistance, prior knowledge, language and device | Give a way to notice, interpret, express and enact the relation |
| What changes in the game? | Entities, rules, state, space, clocks, owners and links | Make consequences actual and available to later play |
| What exists already? | Current files/build/mod set and representative episode | Alter the intended consumer instead of producing an unrelated demonstration |
| What is unknown? | Missing observations and alternatives they would distinguish | Choose a fitting sketch, calculation, enactment or real observation |

These are semantic questions, not mandatory JSON fields. A small scene may fit
on a page; a campaign may need existing data models. Keep game truth, a character's
belief, a player's observation and the designer's explanation distinct. The
canonical character-knowledge procedure is [entities](../../gameplay-design/references/entities.md).

## A worked construction: the Signal House

Original paper prototype. Purpose: choosing whom to trust changes how a group
repairs a public place. No score target is necessary.

- Start: a storm breaks a harbour lamp. Ivo offers a fast repair using a salvaged
  coil; Ren knows the coil was damaged but has not told Ivo. The crew has one dusk.
- Material: one lamp card (dark/lit/unstable), one coil card, a path to Ren's
  workshop, and a public repair record. The crew can inspect, ask, fit or wait.
- Variant A: a published inspection mark lets any crew member verify damage.
  Choosing the coil is a knowingly accepted risk; the record preserves who agreed.
- Variant B: only Ren knows the damage. Asking costs the time needed to inspect
  the safer route. The relation is trust under limited information, not merely
  selecting a larger reward.
- Develop B: Ren can answer only after the crew reaches the workshop; fitting
  before that lights the lamp for one crossing and leaves it unstable. The next
  returning crew encounters the unstable lamp and the signed repair record.

The material now supports a playable choice and a consequence beyond the local
scene. It remains a design hypothesis about trust, not an observed social effect.
If the purpose is a quiet expressive visit, remove the deadline and let players
layer marks on the lamp instead; that changes the relation intentionally.

## Compose a change

For the Signal House, making Ren's knowledge public changes the clue path,
dialogue, cost of asking and meaning of the repair record. Changing only Ren's
line leaves the old dependency broken. Follow an affected relation to its next
consumer, rather than checking every possible system in the game.

Useful cross-domain traces:

- Signal → input acceptance → movement/body clearance → simultaneous threat.
- World fact → discoverable trace → expressible conclusion → speaker knowledge → scene.
- Lost resource or departing member → reachable plan → authority → cost → saved future.
- Source-language feature → player's operation → target presentation → action → later reference.

Each trace needs a common artifact version, entities and clock/ownership semantics.
Local checks on incompatible versions do not establish the combined result.

## Choose, observe and revise

Distinguish a design preference from a factual defect. A coherent scene can be
unwanted; a valued scene can have a broken branch. For an observed difficulty,
consider notice, understanding, ability to express a decision, execution, cost
and significance before selecting the repair. Extra hints address only some of
these causes. Permanent assistance may be the intended participation condition.

Return an actual changed object when change is requested. If the evidence supports
leaving it alone, preserve it and state the reason. When a source premise changes,
update the affected material and the consumer's existing decision record; do not
make the old decision current merely by summarizing it again. General diagnosis,
evidence sufficiency and continuation remain with the bound Assay owners.

## Evidence and transfer

Use this context to preserve framing, construction, dynamics, transfer,
commitment, shared execution and handoff decisions across the task.
The examples are original analytic constructions. These sources support the
distinction between initial ideas, development, framing and constraints; they do
not establish the effectiveness of this agent procedure.

- [Kultima, The Organic Nature of Game Ideation (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1145/1920778.1920784): interviews with game professionals, as examined in CM S01; descriptive practice, not a necessary sequence.
- [Dorst and Cross, Creativity in the design process (2001)](https://oro.open.ac.uk/3278/1/Creativity_-_coevolution.pdf): co-evolution in a small industrial-design study, conditionally transferred to games.
- [Davis, Into the Breach Design Postmortem (2019)](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2019/presentations/Into%20the%20Breach%20Postmortem%20Final.pdf): author account of interacting design, interface and production constraints.
- [Wehrle, Oath Designer Diary 7](https://ledergames.com/blogs/news/oath-designer-diary-7-are-you-in-or-out-victory-part-2): preserving a coalition's purpose while rebuilding its implementation, not a universal instruction to retain every subsystem.
