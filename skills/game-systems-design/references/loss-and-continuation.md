# Loss and continuation

Use this procedure for depletion, death, injury, destroyed production, a lost
role, recovery, retirement or campaign completion. Design the next available
action together with the loss. A deliberate finale is complete when it is the
agreed outcome.

## Choose what ends

Obtain the state before loss, its triggering rule, surviving sources and
abilities, the mandatory cost of the next episode, group obligations, and the
intended horizon. Locate each consequence's bearer: a character can retire
while a person stays in the group and the world retains its history.

| Lost thing | Construct the consequence | Follow the dependency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stock | Deduct named units from a holder | Is its source still available? |
| Equipment or action | Remove or impair use | Does the next required scene need it? |
| Production | Disable a source | Can recovery be funded without that source? |
| Role or authority | End an actor's permission | Who may consent, pay or perform the next step? |
| Character career | Retire the character and settle commitments | Can the person take another role if participation continues? |
| Campaign | Resolve obligations and present a conclusion | What becomes history, and what is no longer promised? |

Keep fictional stress or injury separate from a person's conditions of
participation. The *Blades in the Dark* SRD distinguishes spendable stress,
temporary removal from a conflict, lasting trauma and eventual retirement.
These are game operations, not evidence about human trauma or consent. [BT]

## Construct recovery as play

Write a path from the post-loss state, paying every mandatory cost. Name the
decision available during each interval before full restoration. “Wait until
healed” can be a pacing choice; it does not establish a meaningful role during
the wait.

For repeated loss, include a recurrence and the entry floor. Suppose each
failed expedition costs 2 supplies and fallback work returns 1. A reserve of 4
does not support endless failures: each failure-plus-work cycle removes 1,
and at stock 1 the next expedition's entry price cannot be paid. A repair
route funded only by the destroyed source is likewise unreachable.

Compare recovery with another useful investment in the same budget or time.
Who gives up training to heal someone, who supplies materials, and may either
refuse? Blades downtime rules make improvements compete for limited special
activities and permit extra activities for resources. They do not prohibit
ordinary activity. Transfer the opportunity-cost question, not its exact
schedule as a universal rule. [BD]

## Worked episode: a darkened harbor

This original “Lantern Crew” paper episode starts with a broken generator,
4 crew scrap, no passive income, and two present members: returning Bo and
newcomer Cy. Repair costs 6. Bo owns an equipped winch and 1 personal fuel.
A public survey map is available. The archive route requires absent Ava's
personal pass and active presence; no delegation exists.

The first proposed continuation says: “Buy fuel for 2 scrap, enter the archive,
earn 4 scrap, then repair.” It is unreachable because the pass holder is absent.
Reducing fuel to 1 does not repair that dependency. A positive crew balance
also does not make archive entry legal.

Construct a free recovery episode with these complete rules:

1. Present the public map to both members. Cy chooses **ridge** or **canal**.
   Ridge takes 3 turns and no fuel. Canal takes 1 turn and requires Bo to
   approve spending 1 personal fuel to operate his winch.
2. Before committing canal, Bo accepts or declines that expenditure. On a
   decline, Cy may select ridge. Cy's route decision and Bo's expenditure are
   separate authorities.
3. Complete the chosen route. Ridge reveals **stable landing**; canal reveals
   **damaged pier**. Record that route marker publicly. An abandoned route
   grants neither marker nor payout; spent fuel remains spent unless the
   group's separate event-revision procedure cancels the event.
4. Completing a survey creates 2 crew scrap. The locker now holds 6. Its
   authorized treasurer may spend all 6 on repair or defer that purchase.
   Repair creates a working generator and leaves 0.
5. If working before a survey's completion, the generator creates another
   2 scrap at completion. Repair after this recovery survey grants no
   retroactive generator payout. Starting another public survey has no fee.

Replay ridge: begin `scrap=4, generator=broken`; Cy selects ridge; advance
three turns; reveal `stable landing`; add 2; authorize repair; deduct 6;
set `generator=working`. The final stock is 0. Both members can start a new
free survey; completing it gives 2 survey scrap plus 2 generator scrap, or 4.

Replay canal from the initial state: Cy proposes canal; Bo approves and pays
1 fuel; advance one turn; reveal `damaged pier`; credit 2; repair. The same
repair balance coexists with different route knowledge and personal fuel.
This provides decisions during recovery instead of an automatic restoration.

| Legitimate design choice | Concrete next state | When it can fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preserve severe loss | Repair remains costly; the survey is a smaller episode | Recovery and altered participation belong to the intended arc |
| Start a new crew | Close the old locker; grant an agreed new start; preserve a memorial | World continuity matters more than current resources |
| Complete the campaign | Decommission the base, return personal property, record the final survey and offer no new expedition | Destruction was the agreed terminal event |

A finale needs its ending and settled obligations; it does not require a
rescue grant. A “continue” button leading to an impossible archive route is
a different result.

## Revise the loss without erasing its purpose

If a dead end contradicts continued participation, alter the failed
dependency: an independent source, an alternative task, a loan with specified
repayment, a transferable role, an entry price or a new start. Recheck the
plan this enables and the risk it removes. A refund can eliminate the choice
that loss was meant to create.

For repeated losses use the reserve, recovery rate and cost of another attempt.
For social return use
[access and return](../../game-participation-design/references/access-and-return.md).
For persistent effects of retirement, reset or roster change use
[game state transfer](game-state-transfer.md). Preserve the difference between
group state, an actor's knowledge and a person's willingness to resume.

A paper path establishes reachability under its rules; a runtime replay
establishes the observed implementation path. Whether the vulnerable period
is engaging or acceptable needs evidence of relevant people and timescale.
Do not replace that question with a successful arithmetic check.
